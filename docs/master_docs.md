1. The Idea
Every celebration is an expression of love - birthdays, weddings, anniversaries, festivals and achievements. They are moments when people come together to give, share and show care.
Yet celebrations can also become occasions for accumulating more gifts and objects that recipients may not truly need. GiftHappiness asks a different question: what if a celebration was not only about receiving, but also about transforming someone else's life?
An occasion for celebration is also an opportunity to make somebody else's life a little better.
GiftHappiness allows a person celebrating an occasion to invite friends and family to contribute to a trusted charitable organisation instead of bringing conventional gifts. The celebration remains personal; the gift becomes meaningful; and the happiness extends beyond the people attending the celebration.
2. Vision
To create a culture in which giving becomes a natural part of celebrating. The long-term ambition is not simply to raise money, but to change the culture of gifting.
3. Mission
Celebrations -> People -> Trusted Charities -> Measurable Impact
GiftHappiness will make it easy for anyone celebrating an occasion to replace traditional gifts with contributions to a cause they care about.
4. The GiftHappiness Promise
GiftHappiness is built around a zero-platform-commission principle. It will not take a percentage of charitable donations.
•	No platform commission deducted from donations.
•	The intended structure is for donations to go directly to the selected charity.
•	Only unavoidable bank/payment-processing charges should be deducted where applicable.
•	A strategic objective is to secure a bank, UPI or payment-gateway sponsor willing to absorb or waive transaction charges.
We help without helping ourselves.
GiftHappiness should have a sustainable operating model, but that model should not be built around taking money away from a donor's intended contribution.

6. How It Works
6.1 Create Your Celebration
•	Host name
•	Mobile number
•	OTP verification
•	Address where legally or operationally necessary
•	Occasion
•	Celebration date
•	Personal message
•	Optional photograph
•	Donation page active-from date
•	Donation page active-till date
6.2 Choose a Charity
The host chooses from a curated list of organisations approved by GiftHappiness. Initial cause categories may include children and education, healthcare, poverty and livelihood, disability and inclusion, women and community, animal welfare, environment and conservation, and elder care.
At launch, hosts will not be able to introduce an unverified charity for an individual celebration. This protects the consistency and credibility of the platform.
6.3 Create the Celebration Page
GiftHappiness generates a personalised webpage and shareable link containing the host's name, celebration, date, chosen charity, personal message and contribution period.
6.4 Share
The host shares the page through WhatsApp, email, SMS, Instagram, Facebook or a QR code. QR codes can be especially useful on printed wedding, birthday and event invitations.
6.5 Donate
•	Donor name
•	Mobile number
•	Email if required for receipts
•	PAN where legally required
•	Donation amount
•	Choice of whether the host may see the donation amount
•	UPI, cards, net banking and other approved digital payment methods
The intended payment architecture is direct-to-charity rather than routing charitable funds through GiftHappiness's operating account.
6.6 Thank the Donor
After successful payment, the donor receives confirmation and, where applicable, the charity's donation/tax receipt. A donor may choose to reveal the amount contributed or simply notify the host that a contribution was made.
6.7 Close the Celebration
At the end of the selected period, the host receives a summary of the amount raised and a digital letter or certificate of appreciation from the charity, ideally mentioning the host, occasion, charity, amount raised, date and a brief description of impact.
7. Charity Selection and Governance
Trust is central to GiftHappiness. Proposed minimum eligibility standards include:
1.	The organisation must be legally registered.
2.	It should have existed for at least three years.
3.	It must satisfy GiftHappiness's financial, governance and compliance requirements.
4.	It must have no direct or indirect relationship with GiftHappiness promoters or decision-makers.
5.	It must agree to transparency requirements.
6.	It must provide documentation required for donations and applicable tax receipts.
The final vetting checklist and agreements should be reviewed by appropriate legal and accounting professionals before launch.
8. Equal Opportunity and Charity Rotation
GiftHappiness proposes a predetermined, equal fundraising ceiling for participating charities. Once a charity reaches the applicable limit, it can rotate off the list for new celebrations so other approved organisations receive an opportunity.
Existing celebration campaigns should not be disrupted if a charity reaches its platform ceiling while a page is already active. The charity can become unavailable for new celebrations while existing campaigns complete.
9. Transparency
GiftHappiness intends to display the amount raised for each participating charity and its current status. This provides a clear public answer to the question: where is the money going?
Suggested public fields: charity name, amount raised through GiftHappiness, current allocation/ceiling, and active/completed status.
10. GiftHappiness and the UN Sustainable Development Goals
Every approved charity can be mapped to one or more United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), allowing personal celebrations to connect to a wider global framework.
•	SDG 1 - No Poverty: livelihood and social-support programmes.
•	SDG 3 - Good Health and Well-Being: hospitals, healthcare and medical assistance.
•	SDG 4 - Quality Education: schools, scholarships and educational initiatives.
•	SDG 10 - Reduced Inequalities: disability, inclusion and disadvantaged communities.
•	SDG 13 - Climate Action: environmental initiatives.
•	SDG 14 - Life Below Water: marine conservation.
•	SDG 15 - Life on Land: wildlife, animal welfare and conservation.
Your celebration can become part of a global movement for a more sustainable and inclusive world.
11. Young People and GiftHappiness
GiftHappiness began with a simple idea conceived by a 12-year-old: what if the happiness of receiving could become the happiness of giving?
The youth dimension is important because learning to give early can help children understand empathy, responsibility and the effect their choices can have on others. The identity of the young originator is intentionally not being named in current project materials.
Potential Future Programme: GiftHappiness Young Givers
Schools could encourage students to dedicate birthdays, achievements and other occasions to causes. This may be particularly relevant to IB and international schools where service, community engagement and the SDGs already form part of the educational environment.

15. Technology Direction
Technical options already considered include a Cloudflare Pages/Workers + Supabase database architecture or a self-hosted/cloud deployment using AWS or DigitalOcean.
Launch priorities should be security, reliability, payment integrity, privacy, simplicity and scalability. The direct-to-charity payment architecture should be resolved before the platform is finalised because it affects the overall technical design.
16. Sustainable Operating Model
Potential sources of operating support that do not reduce charitable donations include:

•	Technology/cloud sponsorship
•	Corporate celebration programmes
•	Philanthropic grants and foundation support
Never build GiftHappiness's operating model around taking money away from a person's intended donation.
17. FAQ - Working Policy Positions
Does GiftHappiness take a percentage? No platform commission is intended to be deducted. Donations are designed to reach the selected charity directly, subject to applicable banking/payment costs.
How are charities selected? Organisations must meet predetermined eligibility, governance, independence and verification requirements.
Can a host nominate a charity? Not at launch. Hosts choose from the independently vetted list.
Do donors receive tax benefits? This must be finalised after confirming the applicable Indian tax and donation-receipt structure for each charity and payment flow.
Which payments are accepted? The intended options include UPI, cards, net banking and other approved digital methods.
Are donations refundable? Final wording requires legal/payment review. The platform should account for duplicate, erroneous or failed transactions and applicable regulations rather than publishing an absolute no-refund statement prematurely.
18. Positioning
GiftHappiness is not simply another crowdfunding website. It introduces charitable giving at a moment when people are already intending to spend money on someone else.
Celebration -> Gift -> Cause -> Impact
The behavioural insight is that guests are not necessarily being asked to spend more; they are being offered a more meaningful way to give.
